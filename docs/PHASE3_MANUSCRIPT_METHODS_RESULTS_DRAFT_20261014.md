# Manuscript-ready Phase 3 methods/results inserts (DRAFT, 14 October 2026)

**Editorial status:** Descriptive previously evaluated data. These paragraphs are suitable for further author review, not a declaration of experimental validation. The software is a GC–EI database-connectivity annotation aid, not a stand-alone compound identification confirmation system.

## Methods — post hoc comparative analyses of confidence information

To characterize the contribution of the frozen correctness-estimation model independently from the underlying spectral ranking, the previously evaluated query-level outcome ledgers were reanalyzed without model fitting, threshold revision, or changes to the selected EI retrieval configuration. The fixed search employed nominal `m/z^1 sqrt(I)` cosine similarity against 8,455 source-derived reference connectivity identities. For every query, the binary outcome was whether the original top-ranked reference agreed with the publisher's InChIKey first-14-character connectivity label. This outcome is database-label concordance, rather than authentic-standard-confirmed chemical identity.

Three pre-existing search-observable signals were compared for discriminating correct from incorrect top-ranked database matches: highest EI cosine similarity, the difference between the highest and second-highest cosine similarities, and the frozen 12-feature HistGradientBoosting (HGB) output. Comparisons were made case-by-case on the same 609 originally reserved within-MS-DIAL queries, all of which had already been evaluated before this analysis. Raw cosine and margin values were treated solely as ranking/discrimination signals rather than calibrated correctness probabilities. Brier scores and probability reliability bins were calculated for the HGB output, not for uncalibrated raw signals. For descriptive acceptance–error curves, cases were ordered separately by each signal and accepted at matched case counts, with stable tie resolution. Neither these post hoc acceptance levels nor their observed conditional errors constitute a prospectively certified selective-risk rule.

Nested out-of-fold results on the previously exposed 1,410 development queries were also compared between five-feature logistic regression, 12-feature logistic regression, and the original 12-feature HGB development configuration. Separately, the fixed-model 466-spectrum, previously examined SWGDRUG/RECETOX source-selected ledger was used as retrospective corroboration. The 466 observations represent 419 unique source connectivity labels and have not been individually authenticated for independent sample-acquisition lineage. None of the previously examined cases was relabelled as fresh validation.

## Results — fixed ranking, retrospective correctness discrimination

On 609 previously evaluated within-database queries, the fixed EI retrieval method agreed with the publisher's top-ranked connectivity label for 394 queries (64.70%). The frozen HGB output discriminated correct from incorrect top-ranked labels with an AUROC of 0.8633, versus 0.8127 for the simple top-two cosine margin and 0.7067 for the highest cosine score alone. HGB Brier score was 0.1406 and the 10-bin equal-width expected calibration error was 0.0449. These results concern the confidence of an unchanged search result: HGB did not alter the top-ranked identity or improve the 394/609 retrieval tally.

At matched retrospective acceptance levels, HGB, cosine margin, and top similarity produced 0, 1, and 10 observed mismatches among 38 highest-ranked cases, respectively. At 198 accepted cases, the mismatch counts were 12, 11, and 45; at 305 accepted cases, 31, 41, and 68. Thus, HGB offered better overall ranking discrimination but did not outperform a simple margin screen at every acceptance level. The original unmodified HGB-only historical 10%-target rule accepted 198/609 cases with 12 mismatches. The stricter HGB-plus-margin 5%-target rule accepted 38/609 with zero mismatches; these two historic rules have **not** been certified to control future conditional error, and the strict rule's 38 accepted identities are not identical to the highest-scoring 38 HGB-only observations.

In the historical 1,410-case nested development evaluation, the five-feature logistic, 12-feature logistic, and 12-feature HGB methods yielded AUROCs of 0.8515, 0.8556, and 0.8553, with Brier scores of 0.1484, 0.1467, and 0.1442, respectively. Accordingly, the apparent probability-scoring benefit of HGB over a comparable 12-feature logistic model was small and did not demonstrate unambiguous architecture-level superiority. On the separately previously examined 466 selected-source cases, corresponding AUROCs for HGB, top-two margin and highest similarity were 0.8701, 0.8311 and 0.6988; these values are retrospective descriptive measures and cannot establish independently sourced experimental performance.

In the 609-query ranking error taxonomy, 394 publisher labels appeared at rank one, 95 at ranks 2–5, 20 at ranks 6–10, and 100 below rank 10. The presence of 115 additional source labels among ranks 2–10 illustrates the usefulness of reporting alternative library candidates rather than retaining only a single best hit. Nevertheless, candidate rank and confidence cannot determine whether an unseen authentic compound is present in the reference library, and the findings remain limited by the source labels and unresolved acquisition-lineage independence.

## Software and provenance demonstration

The current version 2.0.2 implementation provides single-spectrum and batch EI input, ranked spectral candidate tables, model-derived correctness scoring, selectable historical acceptance-rule outputs with uncertified-risk disclosures, and downloadable candidate/summary CSV files. The software verifies the original MS-DIAL source checksum, frozen 8,455-reference-index checksum, and serialized HGB model checksum before inference. It does not transfer RI fusion, conformal-set coverage or reference/model parameters from the separate legacy v1 software. The software contribution lies in a reproducible, provenance-aware integration and presentation of the analyses, not invention of weighted EI cosine matching or learned correctness estimation.
